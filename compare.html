Framing contrast

ΛCDM vs EGC

A careful side-by-side of what each framework posits on core topics. EGC is presented here as an alternative research proposal — External Gravitational Forces as the unifying answer — not as a claim that the standard model has been overturned.

EGC overview Proposed checks
Scope of this table

Rows summarise positions as commonly stated for ΛCDM and as stated in the published EGC framing. This is pedagogical contrast, not a comprehensive review of either literature, and it does not invent new observational results.

Comparison table

Topic ΛCDM (standard model) EGC (proposed alternative)
Starting picture Hot Big Bang with an early singularity (or singularity-adjacent origin), followed by expansion of spacetime. No Big Bang singularity. Our Universe (OU) is a finite region in a larger anisotropic landscape — The Universe (TU).
Dark energy / expansion A dark-energy component (often modelled as a cosmological constant Λ) drives late-time accelerated expansion — effectively a push. External Gravitational Forces: pull from masses beyond the OUEH. No dark-energy field; acceleration from closing on external wells.
Central agent Gravity plus dark matter and dark energy (and early-universe physics) jointly shape cosmic evolution. Gravity as the central agent: classical gravitational interaction and self-organization over indefinite timescales.
Redshift / expansion Cosmic redshift primarily from the expansion of space; isotropic on large scales in the baseline picture. Redshift is taken as observed fact; EGC does not commit to expansion vs gravity-well climb vs long-travel (and does not endorse tired-light). Directional redshift patterns remain useful as data for external-pull tests.
CMB Relic radiation from recombination in the early hot universe; anisotropies encode early fluctuations. Not a recombination relic. Proposed as an averaged mass-energy “hum” / filtered field from intervening structure (forest-trunks analogy).
Anisotropy & dipoles Large-scale isotropy is a core assumption (cosmological principle); residual anomalies and dipoles are studied as tensions or systematics. Uneven external wells are proposed to induce anisotropic expansion, CMB anomalies, and features aligned with reported quasar dipoles. EGC’s short paper cites Secrest et al. (2021) CatWISE dipole work as part of that motivation — not as a completed EGC proof.
Cosmological principle Homogeneity and isotropy on large scales underwrite FLRW baselines and a kinematic reading of the CMB dipole. Treated as an assumption under challenge: if OU sits in an anisotropic TU, large-scale directional structure is expected. External papers that report excess dipoles are used as context, not as endorsement of EGC by their authors.
Mathematical / data program Mature pipeline: Friedmann equations, CMB power spectrum, SN Ia + BAO + CMB parameter fits. Exploratory / preliminary maths notes (external potential across OU) plus a distributed reanalysis program (Planck, Pantheon+, DESI/Euclid, JWST, eventual GW anisotropy). Open for testing — not claimed complete here.
Early galaxies / SMBHs Must form within the available time after the Big Bang; high-z discoveries that appear to defy Eddington growth are active tension within that timeline. Read as older edge-dwellers of Our Universe near the OUEH — continuation of The Universe at our limit — not necessarily “impossible babies.”
Beyond the horizon What lies beyond the particle / event horizon is typically not used as the driver of late-time acceleration. Neighbourhood mapping: infer deep wells beyond the OUEH from their imprint on redshift, CMB, and related tracers.
Test posture on this site Established working model with extensive observational infrastructure. Independent alternative proposal with suggested checks (see Predictions) — not presented as completed proof.

Neighbours in the conceptual sense: both frameworks aim to organise the same sky. The disagreement is interpretive — what the phenomenology is of — not a claim that standard data products should be discarded. Primary EGC sources: Reading.
