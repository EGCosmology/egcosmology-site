Questions

  • What is External Gravity Cosmology in one sentence?

    EGC proposes that Our Universe is a finite region in a larger anisotropic landscape (The Universe), and that apparent expansion, redshift features, and related large-scale phenomena are driven by External Gravitational Forces beyond Our Universal Event Horizon — without a dark-energy field or a Big Bang singularity.

  • What do “External Gravitational Forces” answer?

    In this framing, they are the unifying answer to two questions often treated separately: What is dark energy? and Why is Our Universe expanding (and apparently accelerating)? Both are attributed to gravity from mass concentrations beyond the OUEH — pull from outside, not a push from inside.

  • What do OU, TU, and OUEH mean?

    OU is Our Universe (the observable universe) — the finite region we can (in principle) see. TU is The Universe: the larger anisotropic landscape in which the OU sits. OUEH is Our Universal Event Horizon — the effective border of observational access; masses beyond it are still proposed to tug on what is inside. Full definitions: glossary and core vocabulary.

  • Does EGC deny the cosmic microwave background?

    No. EGC does not deny that a microwave background is observed. It proposes a different interpretation: the CMB as a filtered radiative field — an averaged mass-energy “hum” of intervening structure shaped by curvature (forest-trunks analogy) — rather than as relic heat from recombination. A proposed check is whether redshift and CMB variance overlay where external pull is stronger.

  • How does EGC treat dark energy?

    EGC dispenses with a dark-energy field. What ΛCDM attributes to dark energy (including apparent late-time acceleration), EGC attributes to External Gravitational Forces from mass concentrations outside the OUEH — pull from outside rather than a push from inside. Acceleration may ramp as Our Universe closes on those masses. See the comparison table.

  • How is redshift interpreted?

    EGC does not commit to one microscopic cause of redshift. It does not specifically endorse “tired light” / tired-gravity theory. Whether a given line of sight’s redshift comes from an expanding universe, from light climbing out of a gravitational well, from long travel, or from some mix of those — makes no difference to the EGC claim that matters here: the redshift is observationally there, and its directional pattern (together with CMB and related tracers) is what can be compared with the pull of external masses beyond the OUEH.

    In short: EGC uses redshift as data about the landscape of forces acting on Our Universe; it does not require you to pick a preferred redshift mechanism first.

  • Does EGC support tired-light or tired-gravity theory?

    No. EGC is not a tired-light / tired-gravity programme. Redshift’s presence is what EGC needs for its external-gravity picture; the specific photon-loss or expansion mechanism behind that redshift is left open. Expansion, gravitational climb, long travel, or combinations are all compatible with using redshift maps as observational input.

  • Is this claiming the Big Bang never happened?

    EGC does not begin with a Big Bang singularity. Cosmic history is reframed as classical gravitational interaction and self-organization over indefinite timescales within a larger landscape. That is a different origin picture — presented as a proposal, not as a courtroom verdict over the entire standard literature.

  • What about JWST early galaxies and supermassive black holes?

    The framing offers high-z massive galaxies and SMBHs as edge-dwellers of Our Universe near the OUEH — older objects continuing The Universe at our observational limit — rather than as infants that must defy Eddington-limited growth inside a short post-Bang timeline. These are features the model aims to organise — not new measurements invented on this site. See Predictions.

  • Can we map anything beyond the horizon?

    Not by direct imaging past the OUEH, in this framing — but by influence: directional redshift, CMB anomalies, and related tracers may allow neighbourhood mapping of deep wells just outside Our Universe. That is listed among the proposed checks.

  • How does EGC relate to Secrest et al. (2021) and the cosmological principle?

    Secrest et al. (2021) report a mid-infrared quasar dipole (CatWISE) whose amplitude exceeds the expectation from a purely kinematic CMB dipole, and discuss tension with the cosmological principle. The EGC Figshare short paper already cites that work among large-scale anisotropy findings that an uneven external gravitational landscape would naturally organise. That is a citation of motivating literature — not a claim that Secrest et al. endorse EGC, and not a claim that their dipole by itself proves EGC.

  • Is there a mathematical formulation yet?

    Working notes toward a minimal scaffolding exist: an external gravitational potential varying across Our Universe, and a proposed directional correlation between redshift residuals and CMB fluctuations testable with existing maps. On this site that material is treated as exploratory scaffolding and a research roadmap — useful for collaboration and falsifiable checks — not as a finished, peer-validated replacement for standard distance–redshift pipelines. Prefer the Figshare short paper for the scientific core.

  • What datasets does the research roadmap emphasise?

    Collaborating outlines point to Planck (CMB), Pantheon+ and related supernova samples, SDSS / CatWISE-class quasar catalogues, DESI and Euclid large-scale structure, JWST high-z galaxies and black holes, and — on a longer horizon — directional probes of the gravitational-wave background. Each is framed as an entry point for testing EGC predictions, not as a completed reanalysis claimed here.

  • Has EGC been proven?

    No — and this site does not claim that. EGC is an independent research proposal with suggested observational checks (for example a redshift↔CMB overlay). Favourable alignments would be consistent with the framing; discordant patterns would pressure it. Read the short paper via the DOIs on Reading.

  • What about “renewal” or rebirth of Our Universe? (speculation)

    Speculation only — not part of the core EGC claims on this site. Author drafts briefly wonder whether mechanisms (for example involving very massive black holes) could renew hydrogen-rich patches, which might relate to why Our Universe appears “young.” That is exploratory musing, not a required prediction of EGC, and is omitted from the formal abstract. Prefer the published short paper for the scientific core.
