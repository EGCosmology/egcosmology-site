External Gravity Cosmology

A pull from outside, not a push from inside.

A universe beyond our view

External Gravity Cosmology (EGC) begins with one idea. The universe we can see, out to the most distant galaxies, is only one patch of something far larger. This patch, which EGC calls Our Universe, is centred on us because it extends equally far in every direction we look. Beyond its edge lies more matter, spread unevenly. Its gravity still reaches us, pulling harder from some directions than others. Every star and galaxy in our patch feels that pull.

Is dark energy needed?

In 1998, astronomers studying exploding stars found that the universe’s expansion is speeding up. Most scientists credit dark energy, an unseen force said to push space apart from the inside. No one knows what it is, and it has never been detected directly. EGC suggests another answer. Gravity from beyond our view is pulling the galaxies outward. It also offers an alternative to the Big Bang as a starting point, and may explain patterns in redshift, the stretching of light from distant galaxies toward red.

Putting it to the test

EGC is a proposal, not a settled fact, and it can be tested. If gravity from beyond our view is pulling on us, the sky should look slightly different depending on which direction we look. The papers set out tests that today’s telescopes, and those coming soon, can carry out. A clear result either way will show whether the idea holds up.
