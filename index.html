External Gravity Cosmology

A pull from outside, not a push from inside.

The idea

External Gravity Cosmology (EGC) says the universe we can see is only a small part of a much bigger universe. Past the edge of what we can see, there is more matter, and it is not spread out evenly. Its gravity still pulls on everything we can see.

What it could explain

Galaxies appear to be moving apart faster and faster. Most astronomers say this is caused by “dark energy,” an unseen force that pushes the universe outward from the inside. No one has ever found it. EGC says the cause could be gravity pulling from outside instead. Then no dark energy is needed, and no Big Bang as the starting point. It could also explain patterns in how light from faraway galaxies is stretched and made redder, and the uneven spots in a faint glow of microwaves that fills the whole sky (shown below).

How to test it

If this pull from outside is real, the sky should look a little different depending on which direction we look. The papers list tests that telescopes can do now or soon. A clear answer either way would help settle it.
