Papers

What happens if you set aside the Big Bang, dark energy and the idea of an evenly spread cosmos, and look at the sky with fresh eyes? These papers try it, each from a different side of one idea: that gravity from beyond the edge of what we can see is shaping everything inside it. Together they run from the idea itself to the tests that could show whether it stands or falls.

Author: Tom Young. ORCID researcher ID: 0009-0005-4445-6493

  1. Start here

    External Gravity Cosmology (EGC), Short Version

    Why do galaxies seen a few hundred million years after the supposed beginning of time already look mature, and why does the sky look different from one direction to another? This paper asks whether such puzzles are not outliers at all but natural signs of gravity from outside Our Universe, and lists the checks that could test it.

  2. External Gravity Cosmology (EGC), Long Form

    Picture Our Universe as a lump sitting on the slope of a vast valley in the landscape of gravity, pulled outward into an uneven, oblong shape. The long form follows that picture in more detail and with more figures. It also asks whether the edge of what we can see is a hard boundary, or a lens onto what lies beyond.

    Young, T. (2025). External Gravity Cosmology (EGC), Long Form.

  3. Rethinking the Universe: A Conceptual Manifesto of Gravitational Cosmology

    What if the five pillars of modern cosmology are a belt pulled tight around the data, and it has been on too long? This short, informal essay loosens them one at a time and ends with an invitation, not a conclusion: take the belt off and see what shape the data takes.

  4. Toward Testing External Gravity Cosmology: Data and Research Directions

    The tools exist, and the data are already streaming in. This plan breaks the testing of EGC into six projects that researchers can take up one at a time. They run from checking whether the sky’s microwave glow and the stretching of light from distant galaxies line up in the same directions, to a space mission planned for the 2030s.

    Read the PDF

    Young, T. (2025, September 4). Toward Testing External Gravity Cosmology: Data and Research Directions.

  5. Toward a Gravitational Framework for Cosmology: Initial Mathematical Notes

    Most astronomers say light from distant galaxies is stretched because space itself is expanding. These early notes explore another cause: light being stretched as it climbs out of a deep well of gravity. They find nothing in the known laws of physics that rules it out, and offer the idea as scaffolding for others to build on, not a finished theory.
