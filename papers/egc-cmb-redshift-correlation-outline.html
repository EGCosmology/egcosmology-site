Working title

Toward a directional test of External Gravity Cosmology: cross-correlating CMB temperature anisotropy with redshift–distance residuals

Abstract sketch

External Gravity Cosmology (EGC) proposes that Our Universe (OU) is a finite patch embedded in a larger anisotropic gravitational landscape (The Universe, TU), and that apparent acceleration, large-scale anisotropy, and related features arise from External Gravitational Forces beyond Our Universal Event Horizon (OUEH), rather than from a dark-energy field or a Big Bang singularity. A central, falsifiable consequence is that CMB temperature / anisotropy structure and directional residuals in redshift–distance relations may share that uneven external mass landscape.

This outline proposes constructing sky maps of redshift–distance residuals from Pantheon+ (and DESI-style galaxy samples where available), cross-correlating them with Planck CMB temperature / large-scale anisotropy maps, and testing an explicit null of no residual directional correlation after standard systematics control. A robust, bias-aware correlation would be consistent with a shared external-mass imprint; a clean null (or a correlation fully accounted for by kinematics, selection, or dust) would pressure that claim. No finished cross-correlation result is asserted here.

Motivation from EGC

  • Shared external landscape Masses beyond the OUEH are proposed to shape both directional structure in observed redshift tracers and large-scale CMB texture as interpreted in EGC.
  • Primary empirical invitation Already named as a primary proof path in the EGC framing and as a leading item (“CMB and Redshift Correlations”) in the working notes Toward Testing External Gravity Cosmology.
  • Focused test Isolate a single reportable test — CMB ↔ directional redshift residuals — with null and falsification stated up front, rather than opening every dataset at once.

Method sketch

  1. Baseline isotropic fit Fit Pantheon+ (and separately a DESI-style sample) under a conventional isotropic distance–redshift baseline; retain directional residuals on the sky.
  2. CMB preparation Prepare Planck maps with appropriate Galactic / point-source masks; optionally examine several multipole or smoothing scales.
  3. Cross-statistic Estimate a directional cross-correlation (pixel-space and/or large-scale harmonic cross-power) between CMB structure and residual maps.
  4. Significance & robustness Rotation / randomisation nulls; mock catalogues preserving selection geometry; splits by hemisphere, redshift bin, and tracer family; with and without dipole subtraction.

Exploratory working notes elsewhere sketch a qualitative directional target δz(n̂) ∼ δTCMB(n̂). That is a collaboration target, not a fitted amplitude claimed here.

Null hypothesis and falsification

Null hypothesis (H0)

After removal of the kinematic CMB dipole (and other agreed standard corrections), and after control for selection, dust, and Malmquist-type biases: there is no statistically significant directional correlation between Planck CMB temperature / large-scale anisotropy structure and Pantheon+ (or DESI-style) redshift–distance residuals beyond chance, residual systematics, or known local structure.

  • Would support (consistency)

    Robust residual correlation

    A correlation that survives the systematics battery, appears in more than one tracer family, is not fully absorbed by a pure kinematic dipole template, and has a directional sense compatible with a shared uneven external landscape would be consistent with the EGC prediction — not proof that EGC is established.

  • Would falsify / pressure

    Clean null, systematics-only, or kinematic-only residual

    (1) No residual cross-correlation at scales the program is powered to detect; (2) apparent correlation that vanishes under dust, selection, Malmquist, calibration, or mask tests; (3) correlation fully accounted for by the kinematic dipole / local motion template; or (4) SN-based and galaxy-based residual maps that conflict in direction and sign beyond known tracer differences. Falsifying this specific claim would remove the primary empirical invitation named here; it would not automatically refute every other EGC motif.

Systematics

  • Kinematic dipole Solar motion / CMB dipole leakage — template marginalisation; report with and without.
  • Selection & coverage Footprint, magnitude limits, completeness — mask-matched randoms and jackknives.
  • Dust / extinction Reddening correlating with CMB or SN photometry — extinction maps and colour cuts.
  • Malmquist & ladder bias Magnitude-limited residual sky structure — bias simulations; volume-limited subsets where feasible.
  • Calibration & catalogue joins Zero-points, redshift errors, light-curve fitters — alternative pipelines and survey splits.
  • Local structure / bulk flows Nearby anisotropic flows — redshift-bin splits.
  • CMB foregrounds & masks Galactic plane, point sources, SZ — standard Planck masks and enlargements.
  • Look-elsewhere Multiple scales or estimators — pre-register a primary statistic; label secondary tests exploratory.

Not claimed yet

  • No finished detection or non-detection No measured coefficient, significance, or preferred amplitude.
  • No single-paper proof Secrest et al. (2021) and related dipole literature remain motivating context already cited in the Figshare short paper — not EGC proof.
  • No finished theory replacement Exploratory maths notes (external potential Φ; schematic δz ∼ δT) are not a completed substitute for standard pipelines.
  • Mechanism debates out of scope This study uses observed residuals and CMB maps; it does not adjudicate redshift mechanism.
  • No new physics beyond sources Claims stay within the published EGC framing and collaborator research notes summarised on this site.
