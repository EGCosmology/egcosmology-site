About these checks

Each item restates a check already present in the published EGC framing. Favourable alignment would be consistent with the proposal; discordant patterns would pressure it. Nothing here invents new physics, metrics, or claimed completed proofs.

Proposed checks

Current status of the CMB ↔ redshift overlay

Current public-map tests comparing Pantheon+ δμ and CatWISE density with Planck SMICA, Commander, and NILC found no significant overlay: pixel correlations, scale sweeps, and Cℓ comparisons against rotation nulls were all null. The overlay is not treated as an established result. The draft outline records the test scope; other EGC motifs remain open.

    1. Redshift ↔ CMB overlay (directional residuals)

    This was a proposed comparison of directional redshift–distance residuals with CMB temperature / anisotropy maps. Current public-map tests using Pantheon+ δμ and CatWISE density against Planck SMICA, Commander, and NILC found null results across pixel correlations, scale sweeps, and Cℓ comparisons against rotation nulls. The overlay is therefore not treated as an established result. This result applies to the specific overlay prediction; it does not retract EGC as a whole. See the draft paper outline for scope and boundaries.

    What would reopen support: A pre-specified, independently replicated residual correlation that survives the stated systematics and null tests.

    Current reading: The tested overlay is unsupported; other EGC motifs remain open.

  • Proposal

    2. Anisotropy, directional redshift, and dipoles

    Look for directional redshift modulation and large-scale anisotropy consistent with uneven external gravitational wells beyond the OUEH — e.g. faster recession where pulls are stronger. The Figshare short paper already relates this picture to statistically significant quasar dipoles in the literature (including Secrest et al. 2021 on CatWISE mid-infrared quasars) and to CMB anomalies interpreted as signatures of an uneven external landscape — not as mandatory relics of a homogeneous early fireball. Those citations motivate the check; they are not asserted here as proof that EGC is established.

    Would support: Persistent directional / dipole features that fit uneven external pull better than a purely isotropic dark-energy push.

    Would pressure: Robust isotropy at the relevant scales with no residual directional structure of the kind EGC anticipates.

  • Proposal

    3. High-redshift timing (edge-dwellers, not “impossible babies”)

    Assess whether early galaxies and early supermassive black holes at high redshift sit more naturally as older edge-dwellers of Our Universe near the OUEH — continuation of The Universe at our observational limit — than as objects that must assemble inside a short post-singularity clock while apparently defying Eddington-limited growth. Remain open to standard-model alternatives and refinements.

    Would support: Continued tension between observed early structure and timelines that assume a late start from a singularity.

    Would pressure: Standard early-universe accounts that fully accommodate the same objects without residual timing strain.

  • Proposal

    4. Neighbourhood mapping beyond the OUEH

    Use redshift, CMB, and related tracers that may “seep” across the horizon to infer the gravitational neighbourhood just outside Our Universe — deep wells whose sizes and directions leave an imprint on the shape and anisotropy of the accessible bubble. The goal is not to image beyond the OUEH directly, but to map influence: where pulls are stronger, how far we can see, and how the background varies.

    Would support: Coherent directional maps in which pull, redshift, and CMB variance paint a consistent “outside” landscape.

    Would pressure: No stable directional structure usable for such inference after systematics are controlled.

  • Proposal

    5. Survey cross-checks (Planck, Pantheon+, DESI / Euclid, CatWISE)

    Working research notes titled Toward Testing External Gravity Cosmology emphasise Planck, Pantheon+, SDSS/CatWISE, DESI/Euclid, JWST, and longer-term GW. A first focused deliverable from that list is the CMB↔redshift residual outline. Quasar dipole / clustering studies and BAO-as-external-structure readings remain open invitations — not results claimed finished here.

    Would support: Robust, bias-aware residual correlations and dipole structure that fit uneven external pull better than an isotropic dark-energy baseline.

    Would pressure: Null residual correlations and dipoles fully accounted for by kinematics, selection, or standard ΛCDM systematics.

  • Longer horizon

    6. Directional gravitational-wave backgrounds (e.g. LISA-era)

    If Our Universe sits in a lumpy external gravitational environment, next-generation space-based gravitational-wave observatories may eventually constrain directional anisotropy in the stochastic background that correlates with large-scale CMB or structure features. This is a longer-term prediction, listed to show EGC aims at novel future tests — not only reinterpretation of existing maps.

    Would support: Stable directional GW anisotropy aligned with independent large-scale tracers after foreground control.

    Would pressure: High-precision isotropy of the relevant background with no residual directional structure of the kind anticipated.
