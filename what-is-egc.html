Unifying answer

External Gravitational Forces

Gravity from mass concentrations beyond Our Universal Event Horizon — external to Our Universe, still in The Universe — as the proposed driver of what standard cosmology attributes to dark energy and isotropic expansion from a singularity.

Abstract

From the author’s short-version EGC deposit

External Gravity Cosmology (EGC) proposes a revised cosmological model in which the observable universe (OU), or Our Universe, is a finite, evolving region embedded within an infinite, or effectively infinite, anisotropic gravitational landscape, The Universe (TU). Unlike ΛCDM (the standard model of cosmology), EGC dispenses with dark energy and a Big Bang singularity. Instead, OU structure and expansion arise from gravitational forces exerted by mass concentrations beyond Our Universal Event Horizon (OUEH), such as deep gravitational wells distributed unevenly across TU. These external gravitational forces induce anisotropic cosmic expansion, directional redshift modulation, and cosmic microwave background (CMB) anomalies, and organise features that align with recent findings, including early galaxies, supermassive black holes at high redshift, and statistically significant quasar dipoles. The CMB, in this model, is not a relic of recombination but a filtered radiative field shaped by intervening curvature, similar to viewing a forest where everywhere one looks is tree trunk, until depth perception and density come into effect. EGC reframes cosmic history as a product of classical gravitational interaction and self-organization over indefinite timescales and offers a coherent and testable alternative to speculative early-universe physics. It positions gravity, not exotic fields, as the central agent in cosmological evolution, with observational consequences that extend beyond current horizons.

Substance from the Figshare short version (doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29429852). Presented here in impersonal site voice (the deposit itself uses first person); scientific claims unchanged.

Core vocabulary

Three labels keep the framing precise. Confusing them reintroduces “outside the Universe” language that EGC does not mean.

The Universe (TU)

Everything — vast, anisotropic, scattered with uneven mass concentrations that exert External Gravitational Forces. Not required to be homogeneous or bounded.

Our Universe (OU)

The finite light-accessible region we inhabit — an uneven, expanding “bubble” shaped by pulls from outside it. Observers are observationally near its centre by construction of the horizon.

OUEH

Our Universal Event Horizon — the jagged observational border (light’s limit) tugged by external wells. Giants at high redshift sit near this edge.

External Gravitational Forces

The proposed physical agent: classical gravity from masses beyond the OUEH. Uneven pulls → uneven redshift and expansion; no dark-energy field required.

Structured claims (within the EGC framing)

  1. Ontology OU is a finite region inside a larger anisotropic gravitational landscape (TU), not a closed story that begins at a singularity.
  2. External Gravitational Forces — no dark-energy field; no Big Bang singularity Apparent expansion and related phenomenology are attributed to external masses beyond the OUEH, not to a cosmological-constant–like field or an initial singularity.
  3. Uneven pulls and directional effects Uneven external wells are proposed to induce anisotropic effects and large-scale CMB anomalies. Directional patterns in observed redshift are useful tests; EGC does not endorse tired-light and does not require choosing expansion vs gravity-well climb vs long travel as the redshift mechanism.
  4. Acceleration from closing distances Acceleration is framed as gravity ramping as Our Universe closes on relatively static external masses — not as a new repulsive force that grows by fiat.
  5. High-redshift structure as edge-dwellers JWST-era early galaxies and supermassive black holes are offered as older edge-dwellers of Our Universe near the OUEH — continuation of The Universe at our limit — not necessarily Eddington-defying newborns on a short post-Bang clock.
  6. CMB interpretation CMB is proposed as a filtered radiative field — an averaged mass-energy “hum” of intervening structure shaped by curvature (forest-trunks analogy) — not as recombination relic radiation.
  7. Large-scale anisotropy as expected, not incidental EGC treats large-scale directional structure (including reported quasar dipoles and CMB low-multipole anomalies) as signatures of an uneven external gravitational landscape. The Figshare short paper already cites Secrest et al. (2021) on CatWISE quasars in that context — as motivating literature for anisotropy beyond a purely kinematic CMB dipole, not as a completed demonstration that EGC is correct.
  8. Gravity as the central agent Cosmic history is framed as classical gravitational interaction and self-organization over indefinite timescales, with observational consequences that extend to mapping structure beyond the OUEH.
  9. Exploratory math and a distributed test program Collaborating notes sketch a minimal geometric scaffolding (external potential Φ across OU) and a data roadmap — CMB↔redshift residual correlations, supernova distance–redshift re-fits, survey mining, JWST high-z samples, and longer-term gravitational-wave anisotropy. These are program directions, not finished fits claimed on this site.

Glossary

Terms as used in the EGC framing on this site and in the short-version deposits.

EGC — External Gravity Cosmology
The proposed cosmological model in which apparent expansion, redshift modulation, and related large-scale features are attributed primarily to External Gravitational Forces from mass outside Our Universal Event Horizon, rather than to a dark-energy field or a Big Bang singularity.
OU — Observable Universe / Our Universe
The finite, evolving region we can (in principle) observe — treated in EGC as a patch embedded in a larger gravitational landscape, not as the entirety of existence.
TU — The Universe
The larger — infinite or effectively infinite — anisotropic gravitational landscape in which the OU sits. External mass concentrations in TU are the proposed drivers of the phenomenology EGC addresses.
OUEH — Our Universal Event Horizon
The effective horizon bounding what is observationally accessible from within the OU. Masses beyond the OUEH are proposed to still exert gravitational influence on structure and light paths inside the OU; high-z “giants” are proposed to dwell near this edge.
External Gravitational Forces
Classical gravitational influence from mass concentrations in TU beyond the OUEH — the unifying answer, in this framing, to dark energy and cosmic expansion.
Cosmological principle (as challenged literature)
The standard assumption of large-scale homogeneity and isotropy. EGC’s published framing treats reported large-scale dipole tensions (including Secrest et al. 2021 on mid-infrared quasars) as part of the anomaly set that motivates an anisotropic external landscape — without claiming those papers endorse or prove EGC.
Mathematical scaffolding (exploratory)
Working notes toward a minimal geometry in which an external potential varies across Our Universe, with a proposed directional correlation between redshift residuals and CMB fluctuations. Status: scaffolding for collaboration and fitting — not a completed replacement of Friedmann cosmology on this site.
